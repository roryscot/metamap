# Metamap Vision

Metamap should become the trust layer that determines when independently owned
systems are allowed to mean the same thing, act on one another, or inherit one
another's authority.

The goal is not to create a universal ontology. The durable opportunity is to
govern high-consequence correspondences: relationships where a silent mismatch
can corrupt data, widen permissions, invalidate research, or cause an unsafe
action.

```text
map what corresponds
        -> prove what may be trusted
        -> compile only what may safely act
```

## Strategic thesis

The strongest practical direction is to make Metamap a compiler for trustworthy
relationships between systems. Its distinctive boundary is the separation of:

1. possible correspondence;
2. admissible correspondence;
3. executable expression.

Recognition must not automatically become authorization or execution. A system
may recognize two entities as related while still prohibiting their merger,
their use as evidence, or the transfer of authority between them.

## Visionary capabilities

### A constitutional layer for systems

Metamap can serve as a machine-readable constitution between independently
governed systems. It can record what each system may assert, which relationships
require evidence, who may change a shared interpretation, how conflicts are
handled, and when an agreement expires or must be reviewed.

This makes `canonical` contextual rather than absolute: an authority may decide
which projection a consumer accepts without thereby owning the underlying truth.
Contradictory scientific, legal, or historical assertions should remain
attributed and inspectable.

### A correspondence budget

Mappings should carry semantic risk, not only type and cardinality. Exact
identity, broader-to-narrower translation, inferred equivalence, lossy
transformation, and authorization-related correspondence have different failure
costs.

A future relation pack could specify risk classes and consumer tolerances. The
compiler would reject or escalate a mapping when its semantic cost exceeds the
context's budget.

### Counterfactual impact analysis

Before activation, Metamap should answer:

- What breaks if this correspondence is removed?
- Which consumers would silently reinterpret the result?
- Which evidence packages lose support?
- Which policies become too permissive?
- What is the smallest repair that restores consistency?

This extends impact reports from dependency tracing into controlled comparison
of alternative repairs.

### A semantic firewall

The system should preserve a staged commitment boundary:

```text
discovery -> interpretation -> admission -> authorization -> execution
```

Each transition should have distinct evidence and policy. Runtime occupants,
route parameters, or generated bindings must never become a covert way to add a
structural correspondence or widen a capability.

### Executable provenance

Every generation and projection should be able to explain which source facts,
mapping decisions, policies, compiler versions, relation packs, and evidence
dependencies produced it. Provenance should support replay, incident analysis,
scientific reproduction, and AI review—not merely citation display.

### A semantic debugger

An eventual developer experience could let users inspect a disputed
correspondence, step through composition rules, see where information was lost,
compare alternative mappings, and replay an earlier compilation. Diagnostics
should identify the first changed assumption and the obligations that depended
on it, rather than reporting only that compilation failed.

### Typed uncertainty

Confidence should not be a single undifferentiated number. Metamap should be
able to distinguish uncertainty about identity, context, provenance,
completeness, causal relevance, or conflicting authorities. Consumers can then
declare which kinds of uncertainty they tolerate.

### A relation-pack ecosystem

Domain-specific relation packs could govern software APIs, biomedical data,
biodiversity standards, scientific workflows, legal records, manufacturing, and
AI tool permissions. A relation pack should define composition laws,
admissibility rules, evidence requirements, loss semantics, export behavior, and
known failure modes—not just vocabulary.

### AI change boundaries

Metamap is well suited to govern AI-assisted change. An agent might repair
structural references automatically and propose semantic mappings, while being
prohibited from promoting inferred equivalence to approved identity or expanding
authorization through a runtime binding.

The approved generation would become a machine-readable boundary around what an
agent may change without review.

### Organizational semantic memory

Organizations could use Metamap to track important cross-system assumptions:
customer identity, product definitions, financial categories, access meanings,
research transformations, and reporting obligations. A change to one definition
would expose its semantic blast radius and the affected owners.

## Research program

The broader physical-information idea is worth pursuing only through
intervention-based tests. A useful hypothesis is:

> Explicitly governed correspondence structures improve reliability,
> explainability, and fault isolation under change compared with simpler
> dependency graphs or ad hoc rules.

Tests should remove or scramble selected correspondences while preserving the
underlying components, then measure correctness, recovery effort, reviewer
effort, and error localization. The theory earns its place by improving
prediction or performance, not by describing every result after the fact.

## Recommended sequence

1. Make semantic integrity checks for software the primary product surface.
2. Add counterfactual impact reports, provenance replay, typed uncertainty, and
   binding-preservation tests.
3. Validate generality against public scientific mappings, especially existing
   SSSOM-style resources, without erasing their distinctions.
4. Develop relation packs for scientific workflows and synthetic-biology design
   in collaboration with existing standards such as RO-Crate and SBOL.
5. Test the broader theory against strong simpler baselines.

Success should be measured by fewer silent mismatches, fewer false approvals,
lower review effort, better reproducibility, and more precise impact reports.
A system that rejects everything is not trustworthy; it is merely unusable.

## Principal risk

The central danger is overreach. If Metamap tries to encode every form of
meaning, it may become an expensive universal ontology with weak guarantees. Its
advantage will come from governing a bounded set of consequential relationships
and making the limits of its claims explicit.
